Question 1
[6 points · 2 pts each]
A sociologist studies social media use among American college students. A random sample of n = 1,600 students reports spending an average of x̄ = 25 hours per week on social media, with a sample standard deviation of s = 5 hours. Answer the following questions about the sampling distribution of this estimate.
a. Compute the Standard Error (SE) of the sample mean.
Work space…
Answer
SE = s / √n = 5 / √1,600 = 5 / 40 = 0.125 hours
b. Compute the 95% Margin of Error (ME).
Work space…
Answer
ME = 1.96 × SE = 1.96 × 0.125 = 0.245 hours
c. Construct the 95% Confidence Interval (CI) for the population mean.
Work space…
Answer
95% CI = x̄ ± ME = 25 ± 0.245
         = [24.755, 25.245] hours
We are 95% confident the true mean weekly social media hours among all U.S. college students falls between 24.755 and 25.245 hours.
Question 2
[6 points · 2 pts each]
In the same survey of n = 1,600 college students, participants were asked whether they use TikTok every day. Exactly p̂ = 0.40 (40%) said yes. Answer the following questions about the sampling distribution of this proportion.
a. Compute the Standard Error (SE) of the sample proportion.
Work space…
Answer
SE = √( p̂(1 − p̂) / n ) = √( 0.40 × 0.60 / 1,600 )
    = √( 0.24 / 1,600 ) = √0.00015 = 0.012 (≈ 0.0122)
b. Compute the 95% Margin of Error (ME).
Work space…
Answer
ME = 1.96 × SE = 1.96 × 0.0122 = 0.024 (≈ 0.0239)
c. Construct the 95% Confidence Interval (CI) for the true population proportion.
Work space…
Answer
95% CI = p̂ ± ME = 0.40 ± 0.024
         = [0.376, 0.424]
We are 95% confident that between 37.6% and 42.4% of all U.S. college students use TikTok daily.

The figure below shows a standard normal distribution representing the sampling distribution of a sample mean under H₀. The horizontal axis shows z-scores. Mark (or describe) the range of z-scores that defines the 95% confidence interval — that is, the region capturing 95% of all possible sample means.

Standard normal distribution · z-scores on horizontal axis
The 95% CI boundaries are located at z = ________ and z = ________
Answer
The 95% CI spans from z = −1.96 to z = +1.96 (shaded above).
95% of all sample means fall within ±1.96 standard errors of the true population mean. The remaining 5% (2.5% in each tail) falls outside these boundaries.
Question 4
[6 points · 2 pts each]

A researcher compares political attitudes (scale: 0 = very conservative, 4 = very liberal) between urban and rural residents. The figure below shows the sampling distributions of the two group means (blue = Urban, n = 200; red = Rural, n = 200; assumed σ = 1.0).

Sampling distributions of mean political attitude for Urban vs. Rural residents
Q4.1  Based on the figure, are the political attitudes of urban and rural residents statistically different from each other?
Your answer…
Answer
YES — statistically different

The two sampling distributions do not overlap. This means the observed difference between group means is too large to plausibly be due to sampling variation alone.
Q4.2  Would you reject the null hypothesis H₀: μurban = μrural at α = 0.05?
Your answer…
Answer
YES — Reject H₀

Because the two curves do not overlap, the z-score for the difference will be far beyond ±1.96, and p < 0.05.
Q4.3  Is the 95% confidence interval for the difference (μurban − μrural) likely to contain zero?
Your answer…
Answer
NO — CI does not contain zero

When the result is statistically significant (H₀ rejected), the 95% CI for the difference is entirely above or below zero — it does not capture zero. Containing zero would mean "no difference is plausible," which contradicts significance.

A researcher regresses support for universal healthcare (0–4 scale) on two predictors using OLS regression. The output is shown below.

Dependent variable: Healthcare support score (0–4)  ·  N = 1,600  ·  R² = 0.14
Predictor Coeff. (β) SE z-score p-value
College degree (1 = yes, 0 = no) +0.52 0.09 5.78 < 0.001
Age (years) +0.01 0.02 0.50 0.617
Note: H₀ for each predictor is β = 0 (no effect). Two-sided test, α = 0.05.
Q5.1  Do you reject H₀ for college degree? Briefly justify.
Your answer…
Answer
YES — Reject H₀

p < 0.001 < 0.05 (α). The z-score = 5.78 is well beyond ±1.96. Having a college degree is significantly associated with higher healthcare support (β = +0.52).
Q5.2  Do you reject H₀ for age? Briefly justify.
Your answer…
Answer
NO — Fail to Reject H₀

p = 0.617 > 0.05. The z-score = 0.50 is nowhere near ±1.96. Age is not significantly associated with healthcare support in this model.
